Performance Standard 5.3

Three lenses. One responsible choice.

Computing decisions can affect people, communities, and the world around us. Use all three lenses before you build, share, or use technology.

🎯
What you’ll be able to do

Explain how safety, laws, and ethical principles apply to computing situations — and describe a responsible next step.

🛟

Safety

Prevent harm to people, devices, data, and communities. Safety includes physical well-being and protection from digital risks.

  • Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication.
  • Protect personal information; pause before clicking unfamiliar links.
  • Consider ergonomics, screen breaks, accessibility, and online well-being.
⚖️

Law

Laws are enforceable rules. Computing work must respect legal rights, permissions, and responsibilities.

  • Respect copyright, software licenses, and creators’ work.
  • Handle personal data according to privacy rules and school or workplace policy.
  • Access systems only with permission; never bypass controls or misuse accounts.
🧭

Ethics

Ethics means reasoning about what is fair, respectful, and responsible — including when a choice may be legal but still harmful.

  • Be honest and transparent about how a system works and uses data.
  • Look for bias; treat people fairly and make technology accessible.
  • Think about who benefits, who may be harmed, and who is accountable.

Specific U.S. laws discussed in this lesson

These are examples of U.S. federal law behind the copyright, school-record privacy, and authorized-access guidance above. They do not cover every state, country, school rule, or situation.

Copyright Act of 1976, as amended — 17 U.S.C. §§ 106–107

Section 106 describes copyright owners’ exclusive rights, including reproduction and distribution. Section 107 provides a fact-specific fair-use exception; classroom or educational use is not automatically fair use.

U.S. Copyright Office: Title 17, Chapter 1

Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) — 20 U.S.C. § 1232g; 34 C.F.R. Part 99

FERPA and its regulations address access to and disclosure of education records by covered educational agencies and institutions. It is not a general privacy law for every kind of student data.

U.S. Department of Education: FERPA

Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) — 18 U.S.C. § 1030

The CFAA addresses certain computer access without authorization. Whether particular conduct violates the law depends on the facts and applicable law; do not test access to an account or system without permission.

U.S. Department of Justice: CFAA guidance
💡Keep the ideas distinct: laws set legal boundaries; ethics helps us judge what we ought to do; safety focuses on preventing harm. A responsible choice considers all three.

Put it into practice

Pause. Consider. Choose.

For each situation, notice how the three lenses work together. Often, the best first step is to stop, ask, or check a trusted policy.

01 — SHARING

A classmate sends you a paid app

They say it’s fine to copy the installer so your project can finish faster.

Think it through: Safety — could the file contain malware? Law — does the license allow copying? Ethics — is it fair to the creator? Use an authorized source and ask your teacher about permitted alternatives.

02 — PERSONAL DATA

Your project collects student birthdays

You only need the information to personalize a demo, but you have not asked anyone.

Think it through: Safety — could the data expose someone? Law and policy — do you have permission to collect it? Ethics — is it necessary? Use sample data, or ask for consent and collect only what you truly need.

03 — ACCESS

You spot a way into a restricted account

You want to test whether the weakness is real, even though the account is not yours.

Think it through: Safety — testing may expose or alter data. Law — accessing an account without authorization can break the rules or law. Ethics — curiosity is not consent. Stop and report it privately to a teacher or administrator.

Sources & further reading

Official government references for the U.S. laws explained above, plus cybersecurity and professional ethics guidance.

1. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA). “Secure Our World.”

Practical guidance on using strong passwords, turning on multifactor authentication, recognizing phishing, and updating software. Read the CISA guidance.

2. Association for Computing Machinery (ACM). “ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct” (2018).

Professional principles for avoiding harm, respecting privacy, being fair, and following rules for computing work. Read the ACM Code of Ethics.

3. U.S. Copyright Office. Copyright Act, Title 17, Chapter 1.

Official text including copyright owners’ exclusive rights (section 106) and fair use (section 107). Read Title 17, Chapter 1.

4. U.S. Department of Education. Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA).

Official FERPA information and links to its statute and implementing regulations, 34 C.F.R. Part 99. Read the Department of Education’s FERPA page.

5. U.S. Department of Justice. “Computer Fraud and Abuse” (CFAA guidance).

Identifies the CFAA as 18 U.S.C. § 1030 and explains the Department’s prosecution guidance. Read the DOJ guidance.
